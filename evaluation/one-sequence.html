The file as one sequence, measured

The file as one sequence, measured

Oct 13, 202627 min read

Executive summary

On the drawings and texts of Cleaning edited drawings and texts, under the same edits, the one-sequence branch keeps as few live pages as main’s best layout at equal writes, or fewer, and its churn floor reaches further. kladde-rs’s branch one-sequence implements the design of one sequence, which was a proposal when this page was written, in all four of its stages, and ran svg-bench and text-bench at the six settings of the churn floor that main ran, with three bounds L of its span policy. With its default bound, L = 64 bytes, it keeps 1 to 10 % fewer live pages than main’s small layout at equal writes on the tiger, 1 to 2 % fewer on the world map, and as many, within 1 %, on the coat of arms; 2 to 17 % fewer than the packed layout, but for the world map at λ = 1, and 35 to 61 % fewer than the base layout. On the texts, it keeps 2 to 3 % fewer than main where their curves overlap on the 64 KiB text, and as many on the 512 KiB text, where main’s controller had cut its budget to a page. At λ = 0.1, it keeps 1.06 to 1.11 times the drawings’ live size and 1.07 and 1.05 times the texts’, where main reached 1.08 to 1.09 in the small layout, 1.13 to 1.19 in the packed one, and 1.13 and 1.09 on the texts.

The sequence is as compact as main’s small layout, and half its base layout. The drawings’ live size, the sequence’s bytes with the schema chain’s, is 46, 128 and 942 KiB, within 0.4 % of the small layout’s live allocation bytes, 1.3 to 2.4 % below the packed layout’s, and 0.50 to 0.70 times the base layout’s. So the ratios of live pages to live size compare one sequence with main’s small layout byte for byte, and its live pages compare with main’s other layouts on the same drawing without a ratio.

The journal writes 14 to 27 % less, and splices restate nothing behind them. Relative positions shorten a drawing edit’s journal bytes from 37 to 51 per edit in main’s small layout to 31 to 43, and a keystroke’s from 13.2 to 9.6, as the journal’s arithmetic expected. On the texts, the leaves take 6 to 18 bytes of each keystroke’s writes where main’s took 15 to 30, since a splice states one to three entries rather than every statement behind it.

The span policy’s bound decides much of the writes: a merge costs the bytes it writes again, and the garbage it leaves for the cleaner. At L = 256, the proposal’s sizing and the branch’s first default, the cleaner moves 1.6 to 2.1 times the bytes it moves at L = 64 on the drawings and texts at λ = 0.25; at L = 64, every setting of the churn floor writes 4 to 33 % less, for about as many live pages at equal writes, which made 64 the default. At L = 64, the leaves take 8 to 26 % of the drawings’ live pages and 4 to 10 % of the texts’, against 2 to 8 % and 2 to 4 % at L = 256, since short spans keep more entries and more payloads inline; L = 32 traces nearly the same curves as 64.

Edits stay fast, flushes short, and opening fast. At the default bound, kladde’s calls take 39,000 to 123,000 edits per second on the drawings, as many as in main’s small layout or up to 1.6 times as many, with median flushes of 2.2 to 5.0 ms, against 2.6 to 10.8; on the texts, 691,000 to 837,000 keystrokes per second, 14 to 30 % fewer than main, with flushes of 1.9 to 2.8 ms, against 6.8 to 9.2. Reopening an edited file takes 0.8 to 7.7 ms, as on main.

Where it falls short of the proposal: the span table holds two levels only, a span costs about 90 bytes of memory rather than 32, and the cleaner, the evacuation and the walk are main’s, without the refinements the proposal names; kladde-rs’s implementation notes record every deviation.

What was measured

kladde-rs’s branch one-sequence, at b2d0649, ran the benchmarks of Cleaning edited drawings and texts unchanged but for what one sequence removes. The branch starts from defrag-shifts at b1a7f90, as the proposal asked, and takes eight commits: seven that implement the store, the schema, persist, the derive and the facade, the containers, text-bench, and kladde-svg, and one, b2d0649, that sets the span policy’s defaults to what this page measured. kladde-svg has one layout, since every list is a PersistableVec and every string a PersistableString, inline in its parent; text-bench splices the store’s sequence itself, as it spliced one allocation on main.

The edits are the same, edit for edit. svg-bench ran each drawing for main’s number of edits, 31,613, 41,130 and 177,831, with the same seed, the same mix and the same focus, and every run ended with main’s canonical SVG, of 59,005, 184,541 and 1,069,891 bytes; text-bench typed main’s 100,000 keystrokes, and the texts ended at main’s 241,322 and 700,340 bytes. The store flushed whenever the journal outgrew four pages, --journal-budget 4, and at every row, every 1000 edits or keystrokes.

Each workload ran at six settings of the churn floor, with three bounds of the span policy: 90 runs, eight at a time.

The bound is the span policy’s: two neighbouring spans whose entries leave the header for a leaf are merged if they are shorter than L together. rewrite_below is the threshold for overwrites: where an edit cuts into a Ref span shorter than it, the flush writes the span’s bytes again, with the edit’s, rather than split it. It applies to the span being cut, not to the parts a cut leaves, so shorter Refs still arise, from splits of longer spans and from evacuation, as do short inline spans of new bytes; the bound merges those as their entries leave the header, 0.5 to 9.3 bytes per edit at L = 64 in the span policy’s table, so the two constants matter even where they are equal. The sweep ties them to keep its grid small, after tuning runs at λ = 1, which data/ does not keep, had varied them apart: over four workloads, rewrite_below moved the bytes written per edit by at most 7 %, and L by up to 21 %. The span policy’s other constants kept their defaults: inline spans up to 64 bytes, 16 flushes to count as cold for the rotating walk, which looks at 256 spans per flush, and μ = 0.02. The cleaner is main’s churn floor, with its budget controller aiming the file at a fill of 0.8, its free filling, its compaction mode and truncation, over pages of spans.

main is the churn floor’s runs of Cleaning edited drawings and texts, on defrag-shifts at 7eb2ec4, in the drawings’ base, packed and small layouts and on the texts, from the tables in data/cleaning-edits/.

The measures are main’s, with the live size taken from the sequence. The live size is the sequence’s bytes and the schema chain’s, the column alloc_bytes of the tables; live pages are the data pages and the leaves, without the header and the journal, and the branch writes no interior pages of the span table. Space is averaged over the second half of each run, and writes are what the store wrote during that half, per edit or keystroke: the journal’s bytes and every page written, headers included, at 4,096 bytes each. The tables keep kladde-bench’s columns, so that tools/plot-evaluation.py reads them: statements and fragments count the spans, allocations is 0, window_restated counts every entry stated, defrag_rewrites and defrag_bytes the span policy’s merges and the bytes they wrote again, and restated_bytes the bytes of short spans written again where an edit cut into them; columns of the span policy’s own follow.

The runs were built with --release by Rust 1.97.1 and ran on the Intel Core i7-1165G7 of Cleaning edited drawings and texts, four cores and eight threads, 16 GB of memory, under Linux 7.0 in a development container. The benchmarks are deterministic but for their times; the times come from two further passes, one run at a time, of the default bound at λ = 1, 0.25 and 0.1. data/one-sequence/ keeps every run’s table, in a directory named <policy>-lambda<λ>-<layout>, with seq256, seq64 and seq32 for the bounds and one and text for the layouts, logs/ beside them the end of each run’s output, and timing/ and timing2/ the two timing passes’ tables and output, from which the rates, the flush times and the reopen times come.

The trade-off between space and writes

With the default bound, one sequence’s curve lies on or below main’s lowest on every drawing, and on or below main’s on the texts, and reaches further down. Each curve runs through the six settings of the churn floor, from λ = 1, hollow, to λ = 0.1; main’s are dashed, one per layout. Each drawing takes a column: above, live pages over the live size; below, the live pages in KiB, which compare the layouts on the same drawing, whatever their encodings:

  • Against main’s small layout, whose live size one sequence’s matches, the default bound keeps 1 to 10 % fewer live pages on the tiger, 1 to 2 % fewer on the world map, and on the coat of arms between 0.7 % fewer and 0.4 % more, at equal writes. On the tiger the gap closes as the churn floor falls: 10 % at λ = 0.5, 1 % at λ = 0.1.
  • Against the packed layout, it keeps 9 to 17 % fewer live pages on the tiger, 3 to 7 % fewer on the coat of arms, and 2 to 4 % fewer on the world map at equal writes, but at λ = 1 on the world map, where the packed layout, whose writes start lowest, keeps 3 % fewer.
  • Against the base layout, it keeps 35 to 61 % fewer live pages in KiB, since the base layout’s live size is 1.4 to 2 times larger, and 7 to 22 % fewer relative to each layout’s own live size. On the tiger, the base layout writes more at its least than one sequence does at its most, so the curves do not overlap.
  • At its lowest writes, λ = 1, an edit writes 84, 135 and 225 bytes, against 96, 142 and 215 in the small layout and 96, 121 and 164 in the packed one, and keeps 1.39, 1.39 and 1.28 times the live size, against 1.39, 1.43 and 1.34 and 1.52, 1.56 and 1.40.
  • The bound matters less than the churn floor: L = 32 and 64 trace nearly one curve; L = 256 writes more for the same live pages on the coat of arms and the world map, while on the tiger its curve crosses L = 64’s; at the lowest λ, it reaches furthest, 1.05 to 1.09 times the live size.

The equal-write comparisons interpolate main’s curve between its settings, at every setting of one sequence whose writes lie within it.

On the texts, a panel per text gives the live pages over the live size:

  • On the 64 KiB text, the default bound keeps 2.0 to 2.6 % fewer live pages than main where the curves overlap, between 38 and 48 bytes per keystroke, and writes from 30 bytes per keystroke, where main’s least is 37. Below main’s curve, it goes on to 1.07 times the live size at 74 bytes per keystroke, where main’s λ = 0.1 kept 1.13 at 48.

So every setting of the churn floor writes 4 to 33 % less at L = 64, the least on the tiger at λ = 0.1 and the most on the 512 KiB text at λ = 0.1.
  • The spans stay short: 71 to 263 bytes on average on the drawings at L = 64, and 163 and 357 on the texts, against the proposal’s 128 bytes where the sequence is hot and a page where it is cold. The rotating walk merges cold runs, 1.3 to 36.6 bytes per edit on the drawings, whatever the bound, and still the mean span stays below a page by far; this page has not traced which spans stay short, or why the walk leaves them.

  • A splice states one to three entries, and a flush folds the keystrokes at one place into one span: on the texts, a flush states 0.5 entries per keystroke at λ = 1 and splits 0.04 spans per keystroke, whatever the bound.

L = 32 writes 3 to 10 % less than 64 on the drawings, and between 14 % less and 2 % more on the texts, for about the same live pages at equal writes, with 1.1 to 1.4 times the spans; the default stays at 64, since the bound’s guarantee, a mean span in the leaves of at least L / 2, weakens as L falls, and nothing measured here asks for it.

The benchmarks’ processes peak at 3.9 to 6.1 MB of resident memory on every workload but the world map, which takes 15.7 MB, at λ = 0.25, sampled from /proc every 10 ms. The peak comes in svg-bench’s epilogue, which writes the edited drawing’s canonical SVG into a string of 2.1 MB and then reopens the file, decoding a second drawing: 10.6 MB of heap, which with the allocator’s overhead and the mapped binary makes the 15.7 MB.

Most of it could go, and little of it is the store’s. A Box<Element> in kladde-svg’s Node would shrink each node from 328 bytes to 48, and vectors built at their final length would lose their spare capacity. kladde-types’ vector spends 40 bytes on its index of sizes even where it is empty or its elements are fixed-size, 0.86 MB over the world map’s 13,385 vectors: the tree’s cached total and the count’s length need no fields, which leaves the 24 bytes of an empty Vec, or 8 with a thin one; and a Fenwick tree over blocks of 32 elements, scanning up to 31 elements’ sizes within a block, would cost an eighth of a byte per element rather than four. The store’s journal arena should shrink to the journal’s budget after a flush, and its span table and rope could grow in smaller steps, or become a B-tree, which would bring the store’s share to about half a megabyte for this drawing; and svg-bench could drop its copy of the SVG after parsing. main’s memory was not measured, so this page cannot say how one sequence compares with it.

Identical runs differed by more than on the cleaning page. The two timing passes differed in the edit rate by 8 % at the median and 32 % at most, in the median flush by 5 % and 26 %, and in the reopen times by 16 % and 33 %, the largest on the 512 KiB text’s rates and on the coat of arms’ reopens, which take about two milliseconds. The reopen times are medians of 21 opens each.
